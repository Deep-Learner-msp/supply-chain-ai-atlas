# Start here

A learning path for people who know AI but not supply chain, or the other way round.

## Week 1: the shape of the system
1. Read the [lifecycle overview](../README.md#the-supply-chain-end-to-end) and the eight lifecycle pages, in order.
2. Run [implementation 01](../implementations/01-bullwhip-simulator) and change the lead time. You now understand why supply chains overreact.
3. Learn twenty words from the [glossary](glossary.md): OTIF, dwell, detention, lead time, safety stock, S&OP, 3PL, LTL, BOL, POD, and the rest.

## Week 2: the technology fit
1. Read the [fit guide](technology/fit-guide.md). Most failed projects pick the tool before naming the type of problem.
2. Read one survey from [papers](../papers/2025-2026.md): the LLMs and operations research survey is a good first one.
3. Run [implementation 02](../implementations/02-scope3-spend-vs-activity).

## Week 3 onward: go deep on one stage
Pick one lifecycle page. Read its papers, install one of its tools from [repos](../repos/README.md), and try one of its [datasets](../datasets/README.md). Then read its open problems and pick one.

## Formal courses worth the time
- MITx MicroMasters in Supply Chain Management (edX): the most rigorous structured option.
- *Forecasting: Principles and Practice* by Hyndman and Athanasopoulos: free online at otexts.com/fpp3.
